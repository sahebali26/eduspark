1 Basic Concepts
👷 Labour Market
A market where labour services are bought and sold. Firms demand labour for production; workers supply it in exchange for wages.
⚙️ Factors of Production
Basic resources required for producing goods and services: Labour, Land, Capital, Entrepreneurship.
🤝 Labour
Human effort — physical and mental — used in production. Reward = Wages.
🏭 Capital
Man-made resources (machines, tools, buildings) that increase production efficiency. Reward = Interest.
💡 Entrepreneurship
Ability to organise factors, take risks, and make decisions. Reward = Profit.
📈 Derived Demand
Demand for labour/capital is derived from the demand for the final goods they help produce.
📊 Productivity
Efficiency of an input in producing output — how much output is generated per unit of input.
💰 MRP
Marginal Revenue Product — additional revenue from hiring one more unit of input.
MRP = Marginal Product (MP) × Marginal Revenue (MR)
In perfect competition: MRP = MP × Price
2 Derived Demand for Inputs

The demand for factors of production is not direct; it is derived from the demand for the final goods and services those factors help create.

Diagram 1 — The Chain of Derived Demand
Example: ↑ Demand for smartphones → ↑ Demand for workers & machines in smartphone factories.
Key Insight
Labour does not satisfy wants directly — it helps produce goods that satisfy wants. Therefore, labour demand is always derived from product demand.
3 Productivity Concepts & MRP

Marginal Product of Labour (MPL)

Additional output produced by employing one more unit of labour, holding all other inputs constant.

MPL = ΔQ / ΔL

Marginal Revenue Product (MRP)

Additional revenue generated by employing one more unit of labour.

MRP = MPL × MR   |   Under Perfect Competition: MRP = MPL × Price

4 Demand for Labour

A firm will hire labour up to the point where MRP = Wage (W). This is the profit-maximization condition for input hiring.

Diagram 2 — Labour Demand: MRP = Wage Equilibrium
At equilibrium E, the firm hires L* workers at wage W*. If MRP > W, hiring more workers increases profit; if MRP < W, reducing workers improves profit.
Law of Diminishing Marginal Returns: As more units of labour are employed (holding capital constant), MPL eventually falls, causing MRP to decline. This is why the labour demand curve is downward sloping.

6 Competitive Labour Market & Wage Determination

A competitive labour market has many buyers (firms) and sellers (workers), homogeneous labour, and free entry/exit. No single party can influence wages.

Diagram 4 — Wage Determination: Demand & Supply of Labour
Equilibrium wage W* and employment L* are determined where demand (MRP curve) intersects the supply of labour. Any deviation creates excess demand or excess supply that pushes the market back to equilibrium.
  • At W* there is neither excess supply nor shortage of labour.
  • If W > W*: surplus labour — wages fall back to equilibrium.
  • If W < W*: labour shortage — wages rise back to equilibrium.
7 Backward Bending Supply Curve of Labour

At low wages, higher wages induce more labour supply (substitution effect dominates). Beyond a point, workers prefer leisure — the income effect dominates, and labour supply falls.

Diagram 5 — Backward Bending Labour Supply Curve
Up to W_b (backward-bending point), labour supply rises with wages. Beyond W_b, higher wages reduce labour supplied as workers value leisure more.
Remember for Exam
Substitution Effect: Leisure becomes more expensive → substitute leisure with work → supply more labour.
Income Effect: Higher wages mean higher income → "buy" more leisure → supply less labour.
8 Land Market & Ricardian Theory of Rent

Unlike labour, the supply of land is perfectly inelastic (fixed). Therefore, the price of land (rent) is determined entirely by demand.

Diagram 6 — Land Market: Perfectly Inelastic Supply
Since supply is fixed, any increase in demand (D₁ → D₂) raises rent (R₁ → R₂) without changing the quantity of land. Rent is entirely demand-determined.

Ricardian Theory of Rent

Diagram 7 — Ricardian Rent (Differential Fertility)
Rent = output of a given land grade − output of marginal land (C). Marginal land (C) earns zero rent; superior lands A and B earn positive rent due to their higher fertility.
Key Points (Land Market):
• Supply of land is fixed (perfectly inelastic).
• Rent is determined entirely by demand.
• Ricardian rent arises from differences in land fertility.
• The marginal (least fertile) land in use earns zero rent.

10 Factor Share & Income Distribution
👷 Labour → Wages
Paid based on marginal productivity of labour (MPL × Price)
🌍 Land → Rent
Determined by demand; includes Ricardian differential rent
🏭 Capital → Interest
Return to capital based on its marginal productivity
💼 Entrepreneur → Profit
Residual income after paying all other factors; reward for risk

Each factor is paid according to its marginal productivity. This is the basis of the Marginal Productivity Theory of Distribution.

11 Technical Progress

Technical progress improves production efficiency and shifts the MRP curve, thereby affecting demand for inputs.

TypeDefinitionEffect on Inputs
Labour-SavingProduces same output with less labour↓ Labour demand, ↑ Capital demand
Capital-SavingProduces same output with less capital↑ Labour demand, ↓ Capital demand
NeutralIncreases efficiency of both equally↑ Both labour and capital demand proportionally
Technical progress generally shifts the MRP curve rightward, increasing demand for inputs at any given wage rate.
📝 Exam Questions & Model Answers

1-Mark Definitions

Q1. Define derived demand.
Derived demand is the demand for a factor of production that arises indirectly from the demand for the final goods and services it helps produce. For example, labour demand depends on product demand.
Q2. What is partial factor productivity?
Output produced per unit of a single input (e.g., Labour Productivity = Output ÷ Labour), keeping all other inputs constant.
Q3. What is MRP?
Marginal Revenue Product (MRP) = MPL × MR. It is the additional revenue a firm earns by hiring one more unit of labour.

5-Mark Questions

Q4. Explain the Ricardian Theory of Rent.
According to David Ricardo, rent arises due to differences in the fertility of land. Land is fixed in supply and varies in quality. As population increases, cultivation extends from fertile to less fertile land. Rent = output of superior land − output of marginal land. The marginal (least fertile) land earns zero rent; superior lands earn positive differential rent.
Q5. Explain how wages are determined in a competitive labour market.
Wages are determined by the intersection of labour demand (downward-sloping MRP curve) and labour supply. At equilibrium wage W*, quantity demanded equals quantity supplied. If wages exceed W*, there is surplus labour pushing wages down; if wages fall below W*, there is a shortage pushing wages up.
Q6. Explain the backward bending supply curve of labour.
At low wages, the substitution effect dominates — higher wages make leisure more expensive, so workers substitute leisure with work, increasing labour supply. Beyond a critical wage level (W_b), the income effect dominates — workers are rich enough to prefer leisure, so supply falls. The curve bends backward at W_b.

10-Mark Question

Q7. Explain demand for labour in a perfectly competitive market when (i) labour is the only variable factor and (ii) several variable factors.
(i) One Variable Factor (Labour only): The firm's labour demand curve is its MRP curve. MRP = MPL × Price. The firm hires labour until MRP = Wage. Since MPL diminishes with employment, MRP falls, giving a downward-sloping demand curve.

(ii) Several Variable Factors: When capital and other factors can also vary, the analysis becomes more complex. A wage fall has two effects: (a) the substitution effect — firms substitute labour for capital, increasing labour demand; (b) the output effect — lower costs enable higher production, further increasing labour demand. The labour demand curve is therefore more elastic when multiple factors are variable. Firms choose the least-cost combination of inputs to maximize profit.

🧠 Quick Revision — Key Points

  • Demand for labour is derived from demand for final goods and services.
  • MRP = MPL × Price (under perfect competition). This is the firm's labour demand curve.
  • Profit maximization: hire labour until MRP = Wage. If MRP > W → hire more; if MRP < W → reduce hiring.
  • The labour demand curve is downward sloping due to the Law of Diminishing Marginal Returns.
  • The labour supply curve is backward bending — income effect dominates at high wages.
  • Supply of land is perfectly inelastic — rent is entirely demand-determined.
  • Ricardian rent = difference in output between superior and marginal land. Marginal land earns zero rent.
  • Minimum wage above equilibrium causes unemployment (Ls > Ld).
  • Government policies: minimum wage, trade unions, skill programs, social security.
  • Technical progress shifts the MRP curve rightward, generally increasing input demand.
  • Factor rewards: Labour → Wages, Land → Rent, Capital → Interest, Entrepreneur → Profit.
